# INHABIT — Artist's statement (max 500 words)

_MPIEA Frankfurt · Call for Entries 2027 · deadline 01 Sept 2026 · first person_

I am a composer and sound artist from Kalush, in western Ukraine. I studied in Lviv and now work between Hamburg and Gießen. For most of a decade I have written and played sound for live performance: theatre, dance, film, installation. None of it is finished in advance. The music gets tuned in the room, in real time, against a text coming apart on stage, or a body moving through a dreamscape, or a collective rehearsing catastrophe. I started as a guitarist and I still work like one, which mostly means listening to what is already sounding and answering it.

SYMBIONT, my main body of work, carries that habit into an instrument. It keeps the structure of an improvising duet and swaps one of the two performers out. In place of a second musician there is an EEG signal, or a plant, or the electrical weather of a room, turned into sound at the moment it happens. Its engine, braino, maps the five brainwave bands onto a moving stereo field in the browser, and runs either offline from recorded data or live while a signal streams in over OSC. The apparatus is deliberately modest: consumer sensors, off-the-shelf parts. The question it asks isn't a technological one. What does a symbiosis sound like, when the human ear is only one of several nervous systems in the room?

The work belongs to a lineage of biofeedback music that runs through Alvin Lucier, David Rosenboom and Richard Teitelbaum. That tradition asked what the body sounds like. My question is narrower and more awkward: what does it mean to compose *with* a living thing instead of *about* it? Authorship gets shared with something that keeps its own tempo and its own thresholds, and that is perfectly capable of refusing.

Under the technical vocabulary, the work is about repair. I make it as a Ukrainian, from a home that has been broken and scattered by the war, so repair isn't a metaphor I reach for. It's the condition I work in. I understand SYMBIONT quite literally as that labour: building a vessel out of sensors, signal and sound, sturdy enough to carry what moves through a living system without breaking it, and without pretending the maker stands outside the circuit.

My method is small and slow. I build little, listen hard, and let the material (a brainwave, a plant's fluctuation, the resonance of a room) decide what the piece can become. What draws me to the Institute is that this listening has reached the edge of what I can verify on my own. I would like to bring these instruments somewhere the body's response to sound is studied with instruments far more precise than mine, and let the two kinds of listening interfere.
