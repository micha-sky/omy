# INHABIT — Artist's statement (max 500 words)

_MPIEA Frankfurt · Call for Entries 2027 · deadline 01 Sept 2026 · first person_

I am a composer and sound artist from Kalush, in western Ukraine. I studied in Lviv and work between Hamburg and Gießen. For most of a decade I have made sound for live performance — composing and playing for theatre, dance, film and installation, where the music is never finished in advance but tuned, in real time, to a text fragmenting on stage, a body moving through a dreamscape, a collective rehearsing catastrophe. I began as a guitarist, and the guitarist's discipline still governs everything I do: to listen to what is already sounding in a room, and answer it.

My central body of work, SYMBIONT, carries that discipline into an instrument. It keeps the structure of an improvising duet exactly — and replaces one of the two performers with a living system. In place of a second musician there is an EEG signal, a plant, or the electrical weather of a room, rendered as sound the moment it happens. Its engine, braino, maps the five brainwave bands to a moving stereo field in the browser, running either offline from recorded data or live while a signal streams in over OSC. The apparatus is deliberately modest — consumer sensors, off-the-shelf parts — because the question it asks is not technological but relational: what does a symbiosis sound like when the human ear is only one of several nervous systems in the room?

I place this work in the lineage of biofeedback music — Alvin Lucier, David Rosenboom, Richard Teitelbaum — but where that tradition asked what the body sounds like, I am asking what it means to compose *with* a living thing rather than *about* it: to treat authorship as something shared with a system that has its own tempo, its own thresholds, its own refusals.

Underneath the technical vocabulary the work is animated by repair. In the Kabbalistic idea of Tikkun, repair is the mending of vessels that shattered because they could not hold the light that passed through them. I make this work as a Ukrainian, from a home that has itself been shattered and scattered by war; repair is not a metaphor I reach toward but the condition I work in. I understand SYMBIONT literally as that labour — building a vessel of sensors, signal and sound sturdy enough to carry what moves through a living system without breaking it, and without pretending the maker stands outside the circuit.

My method is simple: I build small, listen closely, and let the material — a brainwave, a plant's fluctuation, a room's resonance — decide what the piece can become. I am drawn to the Institute because that listening has reached the edge of what I can verify alone. I want to bring these instruments to a place where the body's response to sound is studied with instruments far more precise than mine, and let the two kinds of listening interfere.
